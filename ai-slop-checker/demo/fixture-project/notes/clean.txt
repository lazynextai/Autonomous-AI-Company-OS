# Field notes — 14 June

The tide was low at six. We carried the two wooden crates to the end of the
jetty, where the water is clear enough to see the ribs of an old wreck under
the sand. Nisha checked the straps twice before we lifted the first crate
into the boat.

The outboard started on the third pull. We crossed the channel at half
throttle, past the red buoy and the fishing boats coming in with their
morning catch. By eight the sun was over the dunes and the engine had
settled into a steady hum.

We anchored in the bay behind the point. The water was calm, the crates
were dry, and the radio played a station in a language neither of us knew.
